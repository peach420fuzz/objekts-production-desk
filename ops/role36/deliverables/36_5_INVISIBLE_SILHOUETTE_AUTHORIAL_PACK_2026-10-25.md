## 2. Visual argument
**Editorial concept:** The body is an inferred force. A shirt, jacket or silhouette in motion can carry a gesture because gravity, wind, fold direction, weight and the floor contact communicate the missing agent. Ordinary city light and plausible urban scale make the absence perceptible.

This is not another showcase of realistic AI visuals. The distinctive authorial question is **how an image produces a sense of performance without showing a performer**. In framing terms, the body is understood by what fabric does to the world around it. In motion terms, an unsupported fold, a detached shoe or an impossible shadow would collapse the effect.

### EN case description, draft (~150 words)
What lets us read a figure when the figure is no longer visible? *Invisible Silhouette* builds a series of urban fashion images around that question. Garments take positions in space, and the apparent performance is carried by the tension of fabric, the direction of a fold, a shoe finding its support, and the scale of a familiar street. There is no need to invent a fantastic city: the scene becomes strange precisely because its light, architecture and surfaces remain ordinary.

The series treats clothing as a carrier of gesture, not just as merchandise. Each composition tests whether a pose can be felt through volume and a believable relation to the ground. A momentary imbalance, a shift in weight or a fold extending into a movement can imply an absent actor.

This page presents an existing visual series. Technical production methods, image rights and contributors will be stated individually when verified.
