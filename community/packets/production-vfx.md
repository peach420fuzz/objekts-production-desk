# Production/VFX packet · one risky shot before many renders

**First-party method; fictional worked example; public first-party reference.**

**Problem:** a moving product shot has convincing atmosphere but the label changes during a hand occlusion. The label and performance need controlling sources; a mood image cannot replace either authority.

Three decisions:

1. Assign approved product artwork/geometry to product truth, and the approved moving plate to camera, performer timing and occlusion. Record conflicts explicitly.
2. Plan exact typography/product shape through controlled graphics, CG or compositing. Limit generative variation to the permitted environment and atmosphere. This is a proposed layer split, not a model guarantee.
3. Test the hardest contact/occlusion section before producing additional shots. A front product still is insufficient to prove an unseen reverse side or an orbit.

**First PoC:** use only approved synthetic or rights-cleared test sources; evaluate the full short moving section and adjacent edit boundaries. Ask the project's named reviewer to define acceptable label, silhouette, contact, camera and temporal checks. If those fail, change the responsible layer or acquire better source data. Do not invent a universal similarity score.

**Three blocking inputs:** authoritative product views/artwork; rights and allowed transformation; camera/plate and delivery geometry. For a future physical shoot, assign each necessary lens/camera/lighting capture to a real responsible crew member. Remote preflight does not supply a physical crew.

An accompanying offline shot-contract prototype checks declared source IDs, rights/fitness holds, conflicting authorities, overlapping allowed changes, open questions and consent flags. Its best result is `ready_for_human_review`. It does not inspect media, verify licences or authorize delivery/publication. The prototype has 22 local controls; it is not a published Production Desk feature.
