# Material should do something

Texture becomes generic quickly when its only job is to make an image look expensive.

A stronger art-direction question is:

> What can this material force the scene to do?

At objekts we track this as **material interference / material agency**.

## Behavior before decoration

Material can carry action or consequence:

- snow can accumulate, erase information and change silhouette;
- water can distort scale, visibility and reflection;
- fabric can determine motion and body shape;
- smoke can partition space and hide/reveal subjects;
- fire can attach consequence to movement;
- glare can destroy or redirect legibility;
- reflective matter can reorganize the relation between subject and environment;
- synthetic image-matter can invade a recognisable world rather than replacing the whole world.

This changes how a shot is designed.

Instead of “add texture”, define behavior:

- accumulation;
- drag;
- displacement;
- opacity;
- reflection;
- adhesion;
- fracture;
- spread;
- resistance;
- melting;
- folding;
- occlusion.

## Preserve a stable world

Material interference is often stronger when the surrounding reality remains legible.

Snow can still feel like snow.
Concrete can keep its weight.
A fluorescent room can stay banal.

One precise material rupture has more pressure when the rest of the image does not compete to be extraordinary.
