# Source authority: decide which reference owns which truth

A visual-production brief often contains multiple references.

The mistake is to blend them into one undifferentiated “style board”.

objekts assigns references by **authority domain**.

## Example authority map

A product packshot may own:

- exact geometry;
- proportions;
- label text;
- logo placement;
- cap / closure construction;
- material finish.

A character master may own:

- face structure;
- body proportions;
- hair;
- age;
- distinguishing marks.

A camera reference may own:

- focal-length family;
- camera height;
- perspective behavior;
- distance to subject;
- motion character.

A lighting reference may own:

- key direction;
- contrast;
- source hardness;
- falloff;
- atmosphere.

A mood reference may own:

- emotional temperature;
- density;
- pacing;
- broad spatial feeling.

## Conflict rule

A lower-authority mood reference should not overwrite a higher-authority product, identity, logo, typography or geometry source.

When references conflict, resolve the conflict explicitly instead of averaging them.

## Why this matters in generative production

Generative systems are good at combining cues. That strength becomes a failure when exact truth is mixed with suggestive style.

The production system should know:

```
which source owns this element?
what is locked?
what may drift?
what method is allowed to rebuild it?
```

This is one of the central principles behind objekts Production Desk.
