# Prove the expensive uncertainty first

A proof of concept is useful when it collapses uncertainty before production scale.

It is weak when it merely produces another attractive image.

objekts chooses PoCs around the failure that would be most expensive to discover late.

## High-value PoC targets

Examples:

- exact product geometry under motion;
- character identity across difficult angles;
- wardrobe continuity across a sequence;
- contact and occlusion between generated elements;
- typography inside unstable imagery;
- camera motion across weak geometry;
- architecture surviving parallax;
- a loop reset;
- a transformation between two locked endpoint states;
- optical integration between source footage and generated material.

## PoC question

A good PoC should answer something binary enough to act on:

> Can this production route preserve the thing we cannot afford to lose?

If yes, scale.

If no, change the route, source, authority or camera before spending on the full sequence.

## Do not brute-force structural failure

Repeated sampling is useful for variation.

It is not a substitute for a wrong production method.

If exact geometry, copy or identity repeatedly fails under uncontrolled generation, move that element to a controlled method.

This principle is embedded in objekts Production Desk feasibility and diagnosis.
