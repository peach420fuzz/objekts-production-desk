# Client surface is not the same thing as studio authorship

A portfolio contains at least two layers of visual information.

One belongs to the **project**:
- client brand;
- campaign world;
- category codes;
- supplied key visual;
- product design;
- licensed IP;
- director/client references;
- required format.

Another may belong to the **studio**:
- how information is withheld;
- how bodies are staged;
- how material behaves;
- how ordinary reality is preserved;
- how image states collide;
- how continuity is controlled;
- how spatial pressure is constructed.

Confusing these layers produces weak “house style” claims.

A warm lifestyle commercial does not prove the studio is warm.
A technical automotive film does not prove the studio is cyan.
A winter project does not prove the studio is snowy.
An abstract R&D piece does not prove the studio is abstract.

## Falsification test

Before calling something a recurring authorial trait, ask:

1. Does it appear in unrelated projects?
2. Does it survive a different client palette?
3. Does it survive a different medium or tool?
4. Can the same decision be described without relying on subject matter?
5. Is there enough provenance to attribute the decision to the studio?

If the answer is no, keep it project-specific.

This is why objekts describes its current visual research through operations rather than one permanent look.
