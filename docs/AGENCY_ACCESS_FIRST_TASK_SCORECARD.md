## The buying decision

An agency often needs to answer a specific visual question before proposing or commissioning a larger film: Can the product stay true to the approved pack? Can the same character survive three angles? Can a camera move, filmed plate and new digital environment maintain one spatial logic? Will the final shot be usable in the edit?

These are **testable production uncertainties**, not requests for an arbitrary number of AI images.

The useful first engagement is a **bounded task with a defined acceptance gate**. Agency Access is a human-led external visual-development desk: a scoped 30-day pilot, **one agreed active task at a time**, subsequent requests in a queue. Finished commercials, substantial production sequences and external expenses are scoped separately. The agency remains the creative lead and owns the end-client relationship. ## The visual feasibility gate

Use a simple **PASS / FAIL / NOT YET TESTED** state. An attractive preview does not automatically pass.

1. **Product geometry.** Silhouette, scale, proportions, label position and typography survive close-ups, rotations and perspective changes.
2. **Character continuity.** Face structure, hairline, wardrobe geometry, body proportions, gait and blocking do not shift between required shots.
3. **Camera logic.** Focal length, height, axis, horizon, perspective convergence, eyelines and parallax remain consistent with the approved setup.
4. **Lighting and materials.** Motivated sources, contact shadows, gloss, translucency, roughness and exposure separate correctly.
5. **Motion physics.** Weight, contact, inertia, deformation, hand-object interaction and event timing read as one causal action.
6. **VFX integration.** The source plate, tracked geometry, occlusion, edge treatment, depth, grain, blur and reflections agree.
7. **Editorial function.** Duration, action legibility, lead-in/out frames and transitions work in the intended sequence.
8. **Output and rights.** The test can be delivered in the required format without unresolved usage, likeness, footage or asset permissions.

Record the failure evidence as *frame/timecode + violated lock + severity + proposed next test*. A failed PoC can still be commercially valuable if it disproves an expensive production assumption early.

## Three useful first tasks

### A — Product-fidelity PoC

**Trigger:** The hero product must appear physically consistent through a difficult move.

**Input:** Approved product dimensions and pack artwork, stills or turntable, intended aspect ratio and two critical camera states.

**Pass:** Stable silhouette and graphic proportions at both states; material and reflection behavior matches the agreed references. Do not infer manufacturer approval from visual similarity alone.

**Output:** A scoped preview with frame-to-reference annotations and an explicit list of unresolved limits.

### B — Multi-shot scene continuity

**Trigger:** A treatment depends on the same performer, wardrobe and location surviving several connected shots.

**Input:** Approved identity/wardrobe/location canon, scene geography, planned shot order and blocking.

**Pass:** Required identity markers, location topology, screen direction, lighting motivation and edit points remain coherent across the agreed sequence.

**Output:** A short proof sequence or animatic, keyframes and continuity issue log.

### C — Hybrid plate / environment extension

**Trigger:** Real filmed action must stay intact while a background, set extension or visual effect is changed.

**Input:** Approved plates, camera/lens metadata where available, protected foreground elements, motion and occlusion references.

**Pass:** Protected source action is not silently rewritten; edges, contact, occlusion, parallax and post-production texture survive actual playback.

**Output:** Before/after technical preview with failure crops, rather than a hero still alone.
