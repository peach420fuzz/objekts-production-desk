# Design the shot for the edit

Shot design and edit design should not be separated until the end.

A generated shot has duration, visual density, motion direction and an attention path. Those properties determine whether it can actually be used in the cut.

## Shot-level questions

Before execution, define:

- target duration;
- first readable beat;
- last useful beat;
- main visual subject;
- entry direction;
- exit direction;
- camera movement;
- amount of internal action;
- what must remain visible at the cut;
- what the next shot needs from it.

## Why generative shots often overperform

Generative video systems are good at adding motion.

Production sometimes needs less.

A beautiful five-second movement can be wrong when the edit needs 1.2 seconds of clean readable action.

An image can also become unusable when too many events compete inside the same short shot.

## Hierarchy over spectacle

For short commercial formats, the shot should communicate its main information quickly.

Atmosphere, VFX, material and environment are useful only when they support the hierarchy.

The edit cannot rescue a frame that never decides what it is about.

## Sequence rule

Generate / render / composite toward the shot function, not toward maximum internal spectacle.
