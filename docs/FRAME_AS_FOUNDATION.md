# Frame as foundation

objekts uses the frame as the common decision surface across different production methods.

Whether a shot is generated, rendered, composited, animated, reconstructed from source footage or built as a hybrid, the same questions remain useful:

- What is the subject?
- What information is withheld?
- Where does the eye land first?
- What scale relationship matters?
- What does the lens do to space?
- What does the camera height imply?
- Where does the light come from?
- Which material has consequence?
- What remains ordinary?
- Which element carries the rupture?
- What must remain exact across versions?

This prevents the production from becoming a sequence of tool demonstrations.

## Tool choice comes later

A difficult visual problem can be routed differently shot by shot:

- controlled CGI for exact repeated geometry;
- graphics for exact copy and logos;
- generative imagery for high-variance organic image construction;
- compositing for local truth and integration;
- source assets for client-owned reality;
- animation or motion design when the visual system is graphic.

The method changes.

The frame still has to work.

## Sequence-level implication

A successful hero frame is not enough. Its decisions must either remain stable or transform deliberately across the cut.

That is why frame construction and continuity are treated as one production system rather than separate departments.
