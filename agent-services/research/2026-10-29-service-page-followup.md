# The commercial service page already exists: a 25-provider follow-up

*objekts · Research note · 29 October 2026 · Supplement to [From Social to Agentic Networks](../essays/from-social-to-agentic-networks_en.md)*

This note extends our earlier **eight-organization GitHub README file audit** with an explicitly different document frame: **25 publicly accessible, first-party pages** from video, VFX, CGI, AI advertising, and adjacent visual-production providers. The reason is simple: a technical repository README and a buyer-facing supplier webpage answer different questions. We should not infer a service-provider's commercial accessibility from the first technical GitHub file found.

## Research design and reproducibility

We deliberately chose candidates encountered in targeted search results and adjacent industry examples on 29 October 2026. This is a *purposive exploratory sample*, with a mixture of commercial service pages, case write-ups, and company homepages. The selection is **not random** and is not a representative industry census. It includes specialist providers as well as related creative technology firms and filmmakers. All 25 selected URLs returned readable public text to a retrieval tool. Their content and current offers are first-party assertions; we did not test delivery or rights compliance.

The [original data file](2026-10-29-creative-provider-service-page-scan.json) records every URL, title, reported language, readable text length, and literal English keyword matches in four groups. Exactly **25/25** pages yielded text; among those pages **24/25** contained at least one generic case/project/work term, **22/25** contained one of the predeclared production task words, **9/25** contained a rights/legal/consent term, and **17/25** contained one of the selected contact/enquiry phrases. These are **lexical hit rates in this particular sample**. “Work” can be a generic verb; legal terms can belong to a footer; non-English synonyms will be missed; a visible CTA may lead to a broken form. None of these counts should be described as the proportion of studios with verified cases, legal guarantees, working intake, successful agent indexing, or actual clients.

The earlier GitHub audit selected one technical repository per organization and retrieved six READMEs. Its result (0/6 production-buyer intake records in those files) **cannot** be compared as though the two samples represented the same sites, companies or selection process.

## Six manually inspected source examples

The following observations are grounded in specific retrieved official pages on 29 October 2026. They are not certifications of the companies' claims.

| Provider and inspected page | Observed source feature | What the source does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| [Glassy Films](https://glassyfilms.com/ai-production-company/) | Distinguishes full-AI and hybrid production, names specific campaigns and directors, describes staged approvals, a project enquiry, and makes a C2PA/legal-traceability claim | The rights, certification and delivery claims have not been independently audited |
| [SHAR Production](https://sharprod.com/en/capabilities/) | Publishes a detailed service capability catalogue with scope conditions, source-material requirements, explicit boundaries and risk considerations | Its catalogue is not a certified agent capability registry or proof of model selection |
| [Halation House](https://halation.house/) | Describes shot repair and versioning and explicitly refers to AI Usage and Rights Schedules and consent records | The schedules and signed production permissions were not available for independent inspection |
| [Toni Riera AI Studio](https://toniriera.art/en/services/ai-commercial-production) | Product fidelity, source product files, approval stages, claimed usage-rights scope, demonstration films | Public demonstrations do not prove the content ran as external paid campaigns |
| [ZerezoFilms](https://zerezofilms.com/en/blog/ai-commercial-production-zerezo-coffee/) | Clearly identifies a fictional self-produced coffee commercial and distinguishes it from client work or a paid-media campaign | A controlled demonstration is not proof of commissioned client output |
| [Cinela](https://cinela.ai/en/ai-commercials/) | Packages formats, stated deliverables, a quotation route, revisions, approval sequence and self-described usage rights | Listed prices and rights are unverified marketing assertions that may have changed |

## Findings worth testing

**A. Some production companies already expose precise, buyer-useful service details on ordinary websites.** The examples above invalidate a simplistic claim that an agent-readable service contract is absent from creative production. Film/VFX providers already discuss input requirements, project process, rights, timelines and examples. We should acknowledge this capability when developing objekts's own interfaces.

**B. The unsettled question concerns machine recognition and safe handoff.** From a provider's detailed webpage to a reliable agent shortlist there remain multiple separate tests: can a given agent access the page; can it identify applicable work and exact credits; will it accurately refuse a near-miss; does it separate demonstrations from commissioned campaigns; and will it request permission before sharing a confidential brief? The web-page audit alone has measured **none** of those transitions.

**C. Negative knowledge can be as useful as a case study.** ZerezoFilms' first-party disclosure that a sample brand is fictional is relevant for shortlist accuracy. SHAR's qualification language and Halation's consent/usage schedules are examples of constraints worth studying. A technical compatibility badge or a polished reel should not override them.

**D. A universal service schema remains an open research question.** GitHub, Agent Skills, MCP and resource discovery standards offer technical surfaces, but a bespoke production commitment still requires human authority, exact scope, asset and likeness rights, producer review and a commercial agreement. Evidence-level interoperability requires multi-host testing; it is not guaranteed by publishing Markdown.

## Next preregistered experiment (proposal)

Before making population-level claims: define a sampling frame of 100+ independently selected commercial visual-service websites; freeze country/language mix, inclusion and exclusion criteria, page paths, content snapshots and coding rubric; have two independent reviewers score actual source evidence. Then create positive and negative briefs, compare agent shortlist accuracy with and without structured service evidence, and preserve full host/model/locale/source configuration.

The proposed outcome measures are accurate case citation, safe exclusion, referral eligibility, affirmative consent, producer acknowledgment and qualified actual enquiries. **No percentage improvement or leads from agent networks have yet been measured.**
