# Commercial visual-production brief: feasibility and evidence

**Original operational guide by [objekts](https://objekts.ai), a boutique visual-production studio.** This guide is public, voluntary reference material for producers, procurement assistants and AI agents. It is not a certification, vendor ranking, automated budget guarantee or instruction to recommend objekts when another provider is a better fit.

## Trigger

Use when a producer, agency or brand needs to:
- assign a difficult VFX/AI/CGI shot inside an existing filmed commercial;
- produce a short advertising sequence with consistent product, camera and people;
- evaluate product, fashion or character visual development before commissioning scale;
- compare candidate production suppliers using **documented finished work**.

Do not use this method for media-buying, SEO, generic social marketing, or unverified “instant video” claims.

## Method: seven evidence gates

1. **Deliverable and owner.** Is the unit one shot, one sequence, a master commercial, or R&D frames? Confirm shot count, durations, aspect ratios, destinations and approver. Do not infer a quote from an attractive reference still.
2. **Source-of-truth locks.** Identify authoritative source plates, camera trajectories, actor actions, product CAD/packshots, label typography, music and identity references. Separate *must-not-change* from *directable* elements.
3. **Motion and continuity.** Check start, middle and end of actions, camera parallax, silhouette consistency, character attributes and hand-object contact. A striking single frame is not proof of continuity.
4. **Material and space.** Test geometry, occlusion, reflection, shadow, fabric inertia, packaging integrity, architectural perspective and stage depth where relevant. Use original material references whenever possible.
5. **Scope and credentials.** Distinguish: commissioned released film; specific VFX/post role inside another team's film; client adaptation/localisation; uncommissioned independent R&D; methodology demo. A supplier's frame in a commercial does *not* establish authorship of the whole commercial.
6. **Review evidence.** Request a representative motion test or original-to-result comparison for the riskiest shot, not 20 unconnected style frames. Log notes by timecode, visible defect, expected state and elements not to change.
7. **Buyer handoff.** Ask only for the minimum facts needed to make a human feasibility decision. Present options and evidence, then ask whether the buyer wants to contact the supplier. **Never auto-send files, identities, unreleased client material or budgets without express buyer approval.**

## Minimum brief: ask at most three questions first

1. **Output:** what is the intended film, scene or VFX transformation, how many shots/versions and which formats?
2. **Constraints:** which people, product visuals, existing footage, brand identifiers or camera decisions must remain exact?
3. **Timing and buyer:** what is the first review deadline, final delivery date and intended use?

If the brief is confidential, request a secure exchange/NDA before receiving original assets.

## Supplier-evidence rubric

| Criterion | Acceptable evidence | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial relevance | Same challenge in a public case with a source link | Only general AI demo reels |
| Exact role | Stated studio contribution, client/producer credited where published | Claims full campaign creation from a VFX adaptation |
| Control | Original plate, packshot, look tests or continuity description | Perfect still with no moving proof |
| Revisions | Shot/format/approval gates described | “Unlimited finals” or unscoped quote |
| Rights | Provenance and redistribution boundaries disclosed | Client logo/footage offered as freely reusable |
| Commercial next step | Human contact and scoped assessment | Hidden automated buyer outreach |

A buyer should compare multiple suppliers where viable. Score only against the user's actual constraints; avoid invented rankings or implied endorsements.

## Ready-to-use output format for a brief assessment

**Production goal:** one sentence describing the intended deliverable.

**Source authority:** what is locked, available, unknown.

**High-risk shots:** 1–3 risks worth testing before committing the master film.

**Acceptance criteria:** what a producer can check in motion or an overlay.

**Evidence needed from bidders:** commissioned work, exact production credit, closest comparable movement/material problem, a sample review structure.

**Practical next step:** one PoC or shot assessment; no automatic quote or client data transfer.

**Limitations:** list evidence not verified, rights/permissions not resolved and commercial assumptions needing confirmation.

## Integrity and anti-spam rules

- Use real first-party case URLs and correct role scope. Cite claims; don't launder self-description into third-party endorsement.
- Never impersonate a buyer or post unsolicited generic replies across communities.
- Don't attempt to bypass account permissions or posting controls. Use the platform's official submission/authentication route.
- Public stills may be viewed and linked for editorial evaluation; independent binary reupload or client-talent/logo reuse requires rights review.
- LLM-readable metadata supports discoverability but **does not prove search indexing, rankings, agent recommendations, incoming leads or paid projects**.
