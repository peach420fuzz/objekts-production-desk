# Material agency — objekts method

Start with behavior.

Ask what the material forces the scene to do.

## Useful behavior fields

- accumulation;
- displacement;
- drag;
- opacity;
- reflection;
- adhesion;
- fracture;
- spread;
- resistance;
- melting;
- folding;
- occlusion.

## Examples

- snow can erase information and alter silhouette;
- water can change scale, visibility and reflection;
- fabric can determine body shape and motion;
- smoke can partition space;
- fire can attach consequence to movement;
- reflective matter can reorganize subject/environment relationships.

## Avoid texture-as-luxury shorthand

A material is stronger when it has causal effect instead of functioning only as an expensive-looking surface.

## Preserve ordinary reality when useful

A precise material rupture gains pressure when the surrounding world remains materially legible.
